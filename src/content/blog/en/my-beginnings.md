## My Childhood

As a child, I liked to argue for what was right in order to meet my needs—whether for food or fun. Regarding the latter, I had a huge number of toys and created countless stories; I also sought validation by trying to build upon my friends' ideas.

I was also allowed to watch YouTube on the TV or play on the computer all day. I used to play games on Friv because I wasn't allowed to download anything—though the one thing I _did_ download was Minecraft.

This was my first real challenge: my father and sister liked to tease me about girls. It bothered me, so I had to keep my worlds separate, forcing myself to resolve everything on my own.

Eventually, there were several fights between my parents that led to a court case, and my father stopped living with us.

## My Little Bubble

A year later, my father moved us out of the house. We went to live with one of his aunts, and by the end of the year, we moved in with another aunt.

Mother's Day brought my second challenge: maintaining my identity. I am the son of two specific people, and just because I was being taken in by another family, I didn't want to have to pretend or adapt just to blend in.

My tablet use was restricted, so I created my own forms of entertainment using paper figures, a pen, and adhesive tape—all influenced by the _Dragon Ball_ ideas that surrounded me. I created "Fuis," a fusion of "Luis" and "Fuego" (Fire) (**Fu** + **is**).

## Becoming a Teenager

Although I changed schools due to my parents' arguments—and because I had a habit of playing games even during class—I had already experienced changing schools much earlier; this time, I went to live with my father's aunt.

It was a small city, so making friends was easier, and I’ve kept in touch with one of them. Two things happened midway through the year: first, during a field trip, I was given a small notebook and a booklet. I started writing down the names of all the songs I liked—having discovered so many the previous year—and carried the notebook everywhere so I wouldn't forget them. Since there was no internet, I had to download music during the few moments I could, stocking up for the "dark days" ahead.

Finally, driven by peer pressure, I began planning how to approach a female classmate romantically. Acting on some advice, I started **writing her a letter** that touched on life in general—sharing curiosities that evolved into life advice.

## Stability?

Just as I was finishing school, my father arrived to take us to live with him, as he had secured a more stable job. Sensing what was coming, I asked my aunt to return the paper dolls she had previously taken away from me.

Upon arriving at the new location, my desire to lead a healthy lifestyle grew. However, the ultimate challenge was money; I began to prefer saving and leveraging economies of scale rather than spending just for the sake of it.

Once I started at the new school, I initially played the role of the quiet, reserved student before gradually opening up; I already knew the formula. However, this school was also a church, and we lived in a rented apartment right across the street, which made it very difficult to separate the different areas of my life. To complicate matters, my father was friends with the teachers—my mother had met him when he was a pastor in the same organization, before leaving him due to family issues. I made many friends; it was my first time using social media. However, as a teenager, I also wanted to form romantic connections, so I chose to "study" people using a notebook—mapping out plans, movements, and schedules. One person led me to delve into church life, another into pop culture, and a third—whom I wanted to help with computer tasks—led me to **learn HTML**.

## Let's keep the momentum going!

I actually managed to enroll for the following year, but then the pandemic hit. Although I didn't move right away, I stopped seeing my friends in person. While I tried to stay in touch via social media, **what kept me most occupied—since I didn't have internet access—was learning to program.**

My idea was to recreate the internet by building my own websites, but that didn't last long; halfway through the year, we moved. I transferred to a public school where everything was virtual. We stayed at a friend of my father's place, and since there was internet access, I started playing Roblox and meeting people online, sharing my achievements with them.

## Coming back

At first, I didn't quite grasp what was happening; having had to say goodbye to so many people, my mind couldn't process it all at the time.

Once the news spread, my mother came to pick us up, and I moved again—this time back to the city where I grew up. School was still remote, so I got a mobile phone; that’s how I met Delasama, after my sister introduced me to the game _Guardian Tales_. I also started attending the church where I had grown up, recognizing familiar faces and asking people what my father had been like.

At the end of the year, I got a computer, which reignited my desire to keep programming—even though I still didn't have Wi-Fi. Around that time, the YouTuber @soydalto started uploading courses on **HTML, CSS, and JS**, and two other important things happened:

1. I went to a church camp and met a girl who wanted to help others regardless of the system; we kept talking on social media, but eventually, we lost touch.
2. I met a Salesian priest who spoke to me about philosophy and helped me come to terms with the concept of death; the idea was that, even if there had been resentment, when you love someone, you can choose to remember them by their best moments.

## Time to Step Up

I finished my school years constantly changing environments. This turned me into a computer enthusiast and also helped evolve my principles—it was time to develop them.

The idea was to visit that last girl I was interested in, so I chose to become a youth leader at my church. However, due to financial issues, I started working to pay for university. The problem was that I wasn't allowed to take on the leadership role, and some coworkers suggested we start a savings pool (_pandero_). The goal was to save up money for the trip, but there were many complications.

In the meantime, I started studying React and its frameworks thanks to the YouTuber @fazt. My job was in customer service for a phone company; I met many people but also had to say goodbye to many others. This experience put a heavy strain on my mind—breaking me down over two years as I tried to pass courses on one hand and build projects for my ministry—which was grounded in philosophical principles—on the other.

But the computer I had been bought only had **4GB of RAM**; I couldn't upgrade to Windows 11, and running both the backend and frontend simultaneously caused major issues. Eventually, I was fired for trying to outsmart the cybersecurity team. I then took my father's computer and tried using Arch Linux, discovering Rust and the free software ecosystem. This led to a realization: I could use my web and Rust knowledge to build applications with Tauri.

## The Climax

I tried to see that girl again, following a few leads and making preparations to leave my hometown and start fresh. I traveled to my aunt's place and managed to find her with the little money I had, but things didn't work out. Since my mother wanted me back, I returned home with the realization that I would have to build this on my own. I decided to step back from social life for a year to focus on myself, but then my uncle—who had been a part of my life since childhood—passed away. My entire family gathered in my hometown, but because I had bottled up my grief and withdrawn emotionally, I didn't make a good impression; I failed to show outward signs of sadness over the loss.

## Finally

With more time to refine my ideas, I began watching videos by the YouTuber @midudev, which helped me expand my ecosystem and reinforce my principles while also gaining a better understanding of myself. Mid-year, I had to upgrade my cooling setup because compiling with Tauri was putting a heavy load on my system; I prepared for this by switching my computer to Arch Linux. I also discovered the PollyMC-Continued project, and I am now striving to be more active and share my knowledge, focusing on performance and modern practices.
